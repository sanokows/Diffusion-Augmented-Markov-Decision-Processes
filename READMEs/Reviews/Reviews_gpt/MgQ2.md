# 3) Rebuttal: Unified Framework for DME-RL

We thank the reviewer for the careful reading and thoughtful technical comments. We are encouraged that the reviewer finds the framework unified, practical, and promising for multimodal action distributions. Below we clarify the main theoretical points and indicate how we will revise the paper to make these distinctions explicit.

**(1) Policy-gradient derivation / surrogate vs. classical theorem.**  
We appreciate this point and agree that the presentation should be more precise. Our intention was not to claim that the classical policy-gradient theorem applies naively to the original parameter-dependent objective without qualification. The key statement is that the gradient used by our method is exact for the surrogate objective introduced in the derivation. Concretely, the policy update we optimize is the step-wise surrogate in Eqs. (3)–(4), not a direct gradient of the intractable full marginal policy objective. We will revise the wording to make clear that the result is obtained via a gradient-preserving rewrite / surrogate construction, rather than by a literal direct application of the classical theorem to the original parameter-dependent objective. We will also clarify this explicitly for the learnable-forward setting.

**(2) Learnable forward kernels.**  
We agree that the current exposition does not cleanly separate the fixed-forward and learnable-forward cases. The proof for the fixed-forward case is correct as stated, and the learnable-forward case requires an additional correction term. In the revision, we will make the scope of the main theorem explicit already in the theorem statement and state clearly in the main text which equations correspond to the fixed-forward case and where the learnable-forward amendment enters.

**(3) Proposition 2.1 / connection to SAC and the LV objective.**  
We agree that the assumptions behind Prop. 2.1 should be stated more explicitly, and we will weaken the wording around “direct equivalence.” Our claim is a conditional correspondence under the sampling construction used in the proposition, not an unconditional identity in full generality. At the same time, we would like to emphasize why we think this result is interesting: because the LV objective is not an \(f\)-divergence, its off-policy use does not inherit the usual information-theoretic guarantees of reverse-KL objectives. This is precisely why our paper emphasizes reverse-KL objectives of the form in Eqs. (1)–(4), where the target and surrogate structure is explicit. We will therefore revise the proposition and conclusion to state the assumptions more carefully, while keeping the broader conceptual connection to max-ent RL and GFlowNet-style objectives.

**(4) DME-WPO with learnable forward/reverse kernels.**  
We understand the concern. In our setting, this coupling issue is avoided by construction: the diffusion coefficients are parameterized with distinct parameters for each diffusion step, so the problematic shared-parameter cross-dependencies do not arise in the form described by the reviewer. We will make this assumption explicit in the derivation around the DME-WPO objective. We agree that if one instead used a shared parameterization over diffusion time, then extra cross-terms would appear and the derivation would need to be modified accordingly. **(5) Relation to WPO after dropping explicit natural-gradient preconditioning.**  
This is a very good point, and we will clarify the claim. We do not mean to suggest that our implementation is an exact realization of the original WPO natural-gradient update. Rather, DME-WPO should be viewed as the diffusion generalization of the projected Wasserstein-flow objective, combined with a practical approximation to the preconditioning step. In particular, the KL regularization term in Eqs. (7)–(8) already induces an approximate trust-region / Fisher-type effect. Since even the inverse-Fisher step in WPO arises from a local quadratic approximation, practical implementations are already approximate. Our choice to use KL regularization instead of explicit inverse-Fisher preconditioning is therefore a practical approximation rather than a fundamentally different principle. We will revise the text to state this more carefully.

**(6) Discussion after Eq. (1): upper bound vs. tighter bound.**  
We agree that the current wording is too strong. Minimizing an upper bound does not by itself imply that the bound has become tighter. What we intended to say is weaker: reducing the upper bound improves the optimization surrogate, but this alone does not prove that the gap shrinks. We will revise the text accordingly. More precisely, if the target objective does not decrease while the bound decreases, then the gap must shrink; however, this is a conditional statement, not a general guarantee from upper-bound minimization alone.

Overall, we thank the reviewer for these comments because they help us sharpen the theoretical presentation. In the revision, we will: (i) distinguish more clearly between exact gradients for surrogate objectives and direct applications of the classical policy-gradient theorem, using Eqs. (3)–(4) as the operative optimization objective; (ii) explicitly separate fixed-forward and learnable-forward cases in the theorem statements; (iii) weaken the SAC/LV “equivalence” language to a conditional correspondence; (iv) make the parameterization assumptions behind DME-WPO explicit; and (v) clarify which parts of the WPO connection are exact and which are practical approximations.
