**W1 Policy-gradient derivation / surrogate vs. classical theorem.**  
In maximum-entropy RL, it is standard that a term of the form $\log q_\theta$ may appear in the reward while the policy gradient theorem still applies (Levine, 2018, Sec. 4.1). As we show in App. D.2, L.832ff,
$
\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim q_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\big]=0.
$
Hence we may equivalently write
$
\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim q_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\big]=0 = \mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim q_\theta}\!\big[\nabla_\theta \log q_{\theta^*}(a_t\mid s_t)\big],
$
with $\theta^*$ denoting stop-gradient parameters. Therefore,
$
\tilde r(s_t,a_t):=r(s_t,a_t)-\Tau\log q_\theta^*(a_t\mid s_t)
$
can be treated as parameter-independent in the policy-gradient derivation, since the gradient of the $\log q_\theta$ term vanishes in expectation.

**W3 Proposition 2.1 / connection to SAC and the LV objective.**  
We agree that “direct equivalence” in L.436ff is too strong and will revise it to “correspondence.” Elsewhere in the paper we already use the weaker framing of a connection/correspondence (see L.059ff and Sec. 2.2), and we thank the reviewer for highlighting this inconsistency.

We also want to stress why this result is interesting. Since the LV objective is not an $f$-divergence, its off-policy use does not inherit the usual information-theoretic guarantees of reverse-KL objectives. We believe this is interesting in light of the errata B in Degris et al. (2012), where off-policy actor-critic convergence is not established for the general learned-policy setting, but only for the tabular case. Our correspondence between the LV loss and off-policy RL may help explain this: because LV is not an $f$-divergence, the upper-bound argument no longer applies, and minimizing LV does not necessarily minimize the KL between the policy marginal and the optimal distribution in Eq. (1). We will add this discussion in the revised manuscript.

**W4 DME-WPO with learnable forward/reverse kernels.**  
We understand the concern and discuss it in App. G.5, L.1535ff. In our setting, this coupling issue is avoided by construction: the diffusion coefficients are parameterized with distinct parameters for each diffusion step, so the problematic shared-parameter cross-dependencies do not arise in the form described by the reviewer. See Eq. 48, where $\log q_\theta$ depends on the coefficient at step $k$, while $\log \pi_\theta$ depends on the coefficient at step $k-1$. We agree this assumption should be made more explicit and will do so in the revision. If one instead used a shared parameterization over diffusion time, additional cross-terms would appear and the derivation would need to be modified.

**W5 Relation to WPO after dropping explicit natural-gradient preconditioning.**  
This is a very good point, which we briefly discuss in L.293ff. We do not claim that our implementation is an exact realization of the original WPO natural-gradient update. In WPO (Pfau 2025) , the inverse Fisher is itself only approximated, since the exact inverse Fisher with respect to network parameters is intractable. As discussed in App. F.1, L.1296ff, practical approximations are standard, e.g. KFAC, diagonal, or Gaussian approximations. Since the inverse Fisher arises from a quadratic Taylor expansion of the KL divergence, an additional KL regularization loss as used in DME-WPO can be interpreted as a practical preconditioning mechanism.


**W6 Discussion after Eq. (1): upper bound vs. tighter bound.**  
We agree that the current wording is too strong. Minimizing an upper bound does not by itself imply that the bound has become tighter. The correct statement is: reducing the upper bound either shrinks the bound itself or reduces the objective being bounded. More precisely, if the target objective does not decrease while the bound decreases, then the gap must shrink. We will revise the text accordingly.
