We thank the reviewer for the careful comment. We believe the presentation in the manuscript can indeed be clarified, but the underlying gradient identity is exact.

Consider
$$
D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(q_\theta(a_{0:T},s_{0:T+1}) \,\middle\|\, \pi(a_{0:T},s_{0:T+1})\right)
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\Tau \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)-R_{\mathrm{env}}(s_t,a_t)
\right].
$$
Define
$$
\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
:=
\Tau \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)-R_{\mathrm{env}}(s_t,a_t),
$$
where we keep the $\theta$-dependence explicit. Then
$$
D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right].
$$

Now differentiate this expression. Using the product rule for differentiating an expectation over a parameterized distribution, we obtain
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right]
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right].
$$

The second term vanishes exactly. Indeed, since $R_{\mathrm{env}}$ does not depend on $\theta$,
$$
\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
=
\Tau \nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t).
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right]
=
\Tau\,
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)
\right].
$$
Now conditioning on $s_t$,
$$
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)
\right]
=
\mathbb{E}_{s_t\sim q_\theta(s_t)}
\left[
\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim q_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)
\right]
\right].
$$
For every fixed $s_t$,
$$
\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim q_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)
\right]
=
\int q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,da_t
=
\int \nabla_\theta q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,da_t
=
\nabla_\theta \int q_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\,da_t
=
\nabla_\theta 1
=
0.
$$
Therefore,
$$
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right]
=0.
$$

Thus,
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta(a_t,s_t)}
\left[
\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(s_t,a_t)
\right].
$$

So although $\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)$ is formally parameter-dependent, this dependence does not contribute an additional term to the gradient, because its derivative vanishes in expectation exactly. For gradient purposes, one may therefore replace $\tilde r_\theta$ by a stop-gradient version without changing the resulting policy gradient. This is the standard mechanism underlying maximum-entropy RL derivations.

When the forward diffusion process is also learned, the derivation is almost identical to the fixed-forward case, except that the forward kernel now also contributes an explicit gradient term. The key point is that the surrogate loss remains valid, but its gradient now contains an additional contribution coming from the $\theta$-dependence of the forward diffusion model.

We start from the joint diffusion reverse-KL objective
$$
D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\theta \| \pi_\theta)
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{s_t, a_t^k, a_t^{k-1} \sim q_\theta}
\left[
\Tau \log \frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k, s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1}, s_t)}
-
R_{\mathrm{env}}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf{1}_{\{k=1\}}
\right].
$$

Define
$$
g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
:=
\Tau \log \frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k, s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1}, s_t)}
-
R_{\mathrm{env}}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf{1}_{\{k=1\}}.
$$
Then
$$
D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\theta \| \pi_\theta)
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{s_t, a_t^k, a_t^{k-1} \sim q_\theta}
\left[
g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right].
$$

Now differentiate this expression. Using the product rule for expectations over parameterized distributions, we obtain
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}
=
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right]
+
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right].
$$

The new feature compared to the fixed-forward case is that $g_\theta$ depends on $\theta$ through both the reverse kernel $q_\theta$ and the forward kernel $\pi_\theta$. Expanding this derivative gives
$$
\nabla_\theta g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
=
\Tau \nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k,s_t)
-
\Tau \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$

As in the standard maximum-entropy policy-gradient derivation, the first term vanishes in expectation:
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k,s_t)
\right]
=0.
$$
Hence,
$$
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta g_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right]
=
-
\Tau
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t)
\right].
$$

Therefore the full gradient becomes
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\Tau_{\mathrm{KL}}
=
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\left(
\Tau \log \frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k,s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t)}
-
R_{\mathrm{env}}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf{1}_{\{k=1\}}
\right)
\nabla_\theta \log q_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right]
-
\Tau
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t)
\right].
$$

This is the key difference to the fixed-forward setting: when the forward diffusion process is learned, there is an additional explicit gradient term through the forward kernel.

We now show how this is captured by the diffusion surrogate loss. Consider the surrogate
$$
L_{\mathrm{DME}}(\theta)
=
\Tau
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{s_t,a_t^k \sim q_{\theta^*}}
\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
q_\theta(\cdot \mid a_t^k,s_t)
\;\|\;
\frac{
\pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid \cdot,s_t)
\exp\left(\alpha Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\mathrm{DME}}(s_t,\cdot)\right)
}{
Z(s_t,a_t^k)
}
\right)
\right].
$$

Expanding the KL divergence yields
$$
L_{\mathrm{DME}}(\theta)
\stackrel{C}{=}
\Tau
\sum_{t=0}^T \sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb{E}_{s_t,a_t^k \sim q_{\theta^*}}
\mathbb{E}_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta(\cdot\mid a_t^k,s_t)}
\left[
\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k,s_t)
-
\log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t)
-
\alpha Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\mathrm{DME}}(s_t,a_t^{k-1})
\right].
$$

Now differentiate this surrogate. There are two types of terms:

1. Differentiating through the sampling distribution $q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}\mid a_t^k,s_t)$ gives the usual policy-gradient term.

2. Differentiating the explicit term $-\log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t)$ yields exactly the additional forward-kernel contribution
$$
-\Tau \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$

Since $Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\mathrm{DME}}$ is evaluated at stop-gradient parameters $\theta^*$, it does not contribute additional derivatives. Therefore the gradient of the surrogate matches exactly the gradient of the original learned-forward objective.

In other words, when the forward diffusion process is learned, the surrogate remains valid because its gradient contains both:

- the usual policy-gradient term for the reverse diffusion kernel, and
- the additional explicit gradient term through the learned forward kernel.

This is precisely why the same KL-form surrogate can still be used in the learnable-forward setting: the only change is that the forward model is no longer treated as constant, so its log-density contributes directly to the gradient.

A concise way to summarize the result is the following:

- In the fixed-forward setting, only the reverse kernel contributes gradients.
- In the learned-forward setting, the reverse-kernel term is unchanged, but one obtains in addition the correction
$$
-\Tau \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^k\mid a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$
- The diffusion surrogate loss remains correct because differentiating its explicit $-\log \pi_\theta$ term reproduces this additional contribution exactly.

Thus the learned-forward case is not a different surrogate objective, but rather the same surrogate with an extra gradient contribution induced by the $\theta$-dependence of the forward diffusion kernel.
