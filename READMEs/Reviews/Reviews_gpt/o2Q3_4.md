We thank the reviewer for the careful comment. We agree that the presentation can be clarified, but we want to emphasize that the underlying gradient identity is correct. This is the standard mechanism in maximum-entropy RL; see Levine [1], Sec. 4.1 and Abdolmaleki et al. [2] App. A.1.

Consider
$$
D^\tau_{\rm KL}(q_\theta(a_{0:T},s_{0:T+1})\|\pi(a_{0:T},s_{0:T+1}))
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}
[\tau\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t)-R_{\rm env}(s_t,a_t)].
$$
Let
$
\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t):=\tau\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t)-R_{\rm env}(s_t,a_t).
$
Then
$$
D^\tau_{\rm KL}\stackrel{C}{=}\sum_{t=0}^T\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}[\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)].
$$
Differentiating,
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\tau_{\rm KL}
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}
[\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(s_t,a_t)]
+
\sum_{t=0}^T
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}
[\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)].
$$
Since $R_{\rm env}$ is independent of $\theta$,
$$
\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)=\tau\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t).
$$
Therefore
$$
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)]
=
\tau\,\mathbb E_{s_t\sim q_\theta(s_t)}
\left[
\mathbb E_{a_t\sim q_\theta(\cdot|s_t)}[\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t)]
\right].
$$
For each fixed $s_t$,
$$
\mathbb E_{a_t\sim q_\theta(\cdot|s_t)}[\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t)]
=
\int q_\theta(a_t|s_t)\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t|s_t)\,da_t
=
\int \nabla_\theta q_\theta(a_t|s_t)\,da_t
=
\nabla_\theta 1
=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)]=0,
$$
and thus
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\tau_{\rm KL}
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T
\mathbb E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim q_\theta}
[\tilde r_\theta(s_t,a_t)\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(s_t,a_t)].
$$

So although $\tilde r_\theta$ is formally parameter-dependent, it contributes no extra gradient term because its derivative vanishes in expectation exactly. Thus one may replace $\tilde r_\theta$ by a stop-gradient version without changing the gradient. We agree that this should be stated more explicitly and will revise the manuscript accordingly.

When the forward diffusion process is also learned, the derivation is the same except that the forward kernel contributes an additional explicit gradient term. Starting from
$$
D^\tau_{\rm KL}(q_\theta\|\pi_\theta)
\stackrel{C}{=}
\sum_{t=0}^T\sum_{k=1}^K
\mathbb E_{q_\theta}
\left[
\tau\log\frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)}
-
R_{\rm env}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf 1_{\{k=1\}}
\right],
$$
define
$$
g_\theta:=
\tau\log\frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)}
-
R_{\rm env}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf 1_{\{k=1\}}.
$$
Then
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\tau_{\rm KL}
=
\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[g_\theta\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})]
+
\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta g_\theta].
$$
Now
$$
\nabla_\theta g_\theta
=
\tau\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)
-
\tau\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$
As above,
$$
\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)]=0,
$$
so
$$
\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta g_\theta]
=
-\tau\,\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)].
$$
Hence
$$
\nabla_\theta D^\tau_{\rm KL}
=
\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{q_\theta}
\left[
\left(
\tau\log\frac{q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)}
{\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)}
-
R_{\rm env}(s_t,a_t^0)\mathbf 1_{\{k=1\}}
\right)
\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(s_t,a_t^k,a_t^{k-1})
\right]
-\tau\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{q_\theta}
[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)].
$$
So compared to the fixed-forward case, the only new term is
$$
-\tau\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$

The same surrogate remains valid because it contains the explicit term $-\log\pi_\theta$:
$$
L_{\rm DME}(\theta)
=
\tau\sum_{t,k}
\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t^k\sim q_{\theta^*}}
\left[
D_{\rm KL}\left(
q_\theta(\cdot|a_t^k,s_t)\Big\|
\frac{\pi_\theta(a_t^k|\cdot,s_t)\exp(\alpha Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\rm DME}(s_t,\cdot))}{Z(s_t,a_t^k)}
\right)
\right].
$$
Expanding the KL,
$$
L_{\rm DME}(\theta)\stackrel{C}{=}
\tau\sum_{t,k}
\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t^k\sim q_{\theta^*}}
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta(\cdot|a_t^k,s_t)}
\left[
\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)
-\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)
-\alpha Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\rm DME}(s_t,a_t^{k-1})
\right].
$$
Differentiating,
$$
\nabla_\theta L_{\rm DME}(\theta)\stackrel{C}{=}
\tau\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t^k\sim q_{\theta^*}}
\Big[
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta}
[h_\theta\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)]
+
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta h_\theta]
\Big],
$$
where
$$
h_\theta=
\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)
-\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)
-\alpha Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\rm DME}(s_t,a_t^{k-1}).
$$
Since $Q^{q_{\theta^*}}_{\rm DME}$ is stop-gradient,
$$
\nabla_\theta h_\theta
=
\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)
-\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$
Using again
$$
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)]=0,
$$
we obtain
$$
\nabla_\theta L_{\rm DME}(\theta)\stackrel{C}{=}
\tau\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t^k\sim q_{\theta^*}}
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta}
[h_\theta\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k,s_t)]
-\tau\sum_{t,k}\mathbb E_{s_t,a_t^k\sim q_{\theta^*}}
\mathbb E_{a_t^{k-1}\sim q_\theta}
[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t)].
$$
This is exactly the same structure as above: the usual policy-gradient term through $q_\theta$, plus the additional correction
$$
-\tau\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t^k|a_t^{k-1},s_t).
$$
Hence the surrogate gradient matches the gradient of the learned-forward objective exactly. Therefore the learned-forward case does not require a different surrogate; it is the same KL surrogate, with the extra gradient contribution induced by the $\theta$-dependence of the forward kernel.
