# Review Response Plan: Unified Framework for DME-RL

## Summary
The paper proposes a unified framework for diffusion-based maximum-entropy reinforcement learning (DME-RL), viewing RL as a sampling problem and parameterizing the policy with a reverse diffusion process. This allows for flexible modeling of complex, multimodal action distributions.

---

## Strengths
- **Unified framework for diffusion-based RL**
- **Practical implementation with small changes to PPO/REPPO/WPO**
- **Strong empirical results on benchmarks**
- **Well-motivated and promising for multimodal action distributions**

We thank the reviewer for the careful reading and thoughtful technical comments. We are encouraged that the reviewer finds the framework unified, practical, and promising for multimodal action distributions. Below we clarify the main theoretical points and indicate how we will revise the paper to make these distinctions explicit.

**(1) Policy-gradient derivation / surrogate vs. classical theorem.**  
We appreciate this point and agree that the presentation should be more precise. Our intention was not to claim that the classical policy-gradient theorem applies naively to the original parameter-dependent objective without qualification. The key statement is that the gradient used by our method is exact for the surrogate objective introduced in the derivation, and this surrogate is first-order equivalent to the original objective at the current parameters. We will revise the wording to make clear that the result is obtained via a gradient-preserving rewrite / surrogate construction, rather than by a literal direct application of the classical theorem to a parameter-independent reward formulation. We will also clarify this point explicitly for the learnable-forward setting, where the distinction matters even more.

**(2) Learnable forward kernels.**  
We agree that the current exposition does not cleanly separate the fixed-forward and learnable-forward cases. The proof for the fixed-forward case is correct as stated, and Appendix G.3 already derives the additional correction term for the learnable-forward setting. The problem is therefore not correctness but presentation. In the revision, we will make the scope of the main theorem explicit already in the theorem statement and state clearly in the main text that the simpler derivation assumes a fixed forward kernel, while the learnable-forward case requires the amended gradient from Appendix G.3.

**(3) Proposition 2.1 / connection to SAC and the LV objective.**  
We agree that the assumptions behind Prop. 2.1 should be stated more explicitly, and we will weaken the wording around “direct equivalence.” Our claim is a conditional correspondence under the sampling construction used in the proposition, not an unconditional identity in full generality. At the same time, we would like to emphasize why we think this result is interesting: because the LV objective is not an \(f\)-divergence, its off-policy use does not inherit the usual information-theoretic guarantees of reverse-KL objectives. This has direct implications for off-policy RL, where instability is well known, and may also help explain why LV-style objectives can work in practice yet be brittle. We will therefore revise the proposition and conclusion to state the assumptions more carefully, while keeping the broader conceptual connection to max-ent RL and GFlowNet-style objectives.

**(4) DME-WPO with learnable forward/reverse kernels.**  
We understand the concern. In our setting, this coupling issue is avoided by construction: the diffusion coefficients are parameterized with distinct parameters for each diffusion step, as is common in diffusion samplers, so the problematic shared-parameter cross-dependencies do not arise in the form described by the reviewer. We will make this assumption explicit in the derivation around Eq. (14). We agree that if one instead used a shared parameterization (e.g., an MLP over diffusion time), then extra cross-terms would appear and the derivation would need to be modified accordingly.

**(5) Relation to WPO after dropping explicit natural-gradient preconditioning.**  
This is a very good point, and we will clarify the claim. We do not mean to suggest that our implementation is an exact realization of the original WPO natural-gradient update. Rather, DME-WPO should be viewed as the diffusion generalization of the projected Wasserstein-flow objective, combined with a practical approximation to the preconditioning step. In particular, the KL constraint already induces an approximate Fisher-based preconditioning effect; indeed, the inverse-Fisher step in WPO itself arises through a local quadratic / Taylor approximation, so practical implementations are already approximate. Our choice to use KL regularization instead of explicit inverse-Fisher preconditioning is therefore a practical approximation rather than a fundamentally different principle. We will revise the text to state this more carefully and avoid overstating the faithfulness of the connection.

**(6) Discussion after Eq. (1): upper bound vs. tighter bound.**  
We agree that the current wording is too strong. Minimizing an upper bound does not by itself imply that the bound has become tighter. What we intended to say is weaker: reducing the upper bound improves the optimization surrogate, but this alone does not prove that the gap shrinks. We will revise the text accordingly. More precisely, if the target objective does not decrease while the bound decreases, then the gap must shrink; however, this is a conditional statement, not a general guarantee from upper-bound minimization alone. We will either state this carefully or remove the “tightens the gap” phrasing.

Overall, we thank the reviewer for these comments because they help us sharpen the theoretical presentation. In the revision, we will:  
(i) distinguish more clearly between exact gradients for surrogate objectives and direct applications of the classical policy-gradient theorem,  
(ii) explicitly separate fixed-forward and learnable-forward cases in the theorem statements,  
(iii) weaken the SAC/LV “equivalence” language to a conditional correspondence,  
(iv) make the parameterization assumptions behind DME-WPO explicit, and  
(v) clarify which parts of the WPO connection are exact and which are practical approximations.
